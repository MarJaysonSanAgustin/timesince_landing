The best log is the one you never had to remember to make.

Most habit tracking fails at a very specific moment: you notice you did the thing, and then you would have to unlock your phone, find an app, wait for it to load, locate the right entry, and tap it. That is four or five steps competing with whatever else is pulling at your attention right then. The habit is fine. The logging is what breaks.

So the useful question is not "which tracker has the best design?" It is "how few steps can there be between noticing and recording?"

## Why friction decides this

Every step between intent and action is a chance to drop it. That is true for everyone, and it is [especially true with ADHD](/guides/habit-tracking-with-adhd), where the gap between "I should log this" and actually doing it is where a lot of good intentions quietly disappear.

This is also why an app being *pleasant* matters less than an app being *reachable*. A beautiful interface you have to navigate to is still a navigation. The methods below all share one property: they put the log where the habit already is.

## Three ways in, ranked by friction

**The home screen widget** is the lowest-effort option and the one most people should start with. One tap, straight from the home screen, no unlock into the app. It is on the free tier, so there is nothing to decide before trying it.

**Siri** wins whenever your hands are busy or the phone is not already out. Saying it out loud is faster than finding anything, and it works from Spotlight, the Shortcuts app, and the Action Button if your iPhone has one. This is a Pro feature.

**An NFC tag** is the strangest-sounding and, for certain habits, by far the best. You pair an NTAG215 sticker with a counter and put it on the object itself. The water filter has a sticker on it. The pillbox has a sticker on it. You tap your phone against it and it is logged — no app, no unlock, no decision. The tag costs very little and needs no battery. Also Pro.

## Which one for which habit

Match the method to where the habit physically happens:

- **Something you do at your phone anyway** — reading, a breathing exercise, checking in on someone: the widget.
- **Something at a fixed physical place** — medication, filters, pet treatments, watering: an NFC tag on the object.
- **Something with your hands full or mid-task** — cooking, driving, exercise, leaving the house: Siri.
- **Something irregular and easy to forget entirely** — see [tracking the chores that don't happen every day](/guides/tracking-irregular-chores); the tag usually wins, because the object is the reminder.

## A note on over-engineering this

It is tempting to set up all three for everything. Don't. Each extra path is one more thing to maintain and one more small decision at the moment you are trying to remove decisions. Pick one per habit, use it for a week, and keep what actually fired.

The same goes for how much you track. Two or three habits logged consistently beats twelve logged for a fortnight and then ignored.

## The bottom line

The method matters less than the step count. Whatever gets you from noticing to logged in one action is the right answer, and on iPhone that is a widget tap, a sentence to Siri, or a tag on the object. Everything else is the app's problem, not yours.
