The free tier here is not a trial with a countdown. Three counters, kept indefinitely, with logging, reminders, categories, the home screen widget and private iCloud sync. If three things are what you need to track, you are done, and nothing will start nagging you.

That makes the upgrade question narrower than usual: what specifically do you hit?

## Most people hit the number

Three is enough for a focused set and not enough for a household. The moment you want the water filter, the car, the cat's flea treatment and calling your parents, you are at four, and the cap is the thing in your way.

That is the straightforward case, and unlimited counters is the main thing Pro sells.

## Some people hit the friction instead

The other reason to upgrade has nothing to do with the number. NFC tap-to-log and Siri Shortcuts are both Pro, and they are the difference between a log you make and one you meant to make. If your three counters are physical things in fixed places — a pillbox, a filter, a litter tray — then [a tag on the object](/guides/log-a-habit-without-opening-the-app) may matter more to you than a fourth counter ever would.

Worth separating those two reasons before you decide, because only one of them is solved by the cap going away.

## On the lifetime option

$39.99 once against $14.99 a year means lifetime is cheaper from year three. One App Store reviewer called the lifetime purchase a no-brainer, which is a reasonable read if you already know the app has stuck.

If you do not know that yet, a month at $1.99 is a cheaper way to find out than committing either way.

## When Pro is not worth it

If you track three things, log them by opening the app, and do not need anything hidden, Pro changes nothing about your day. The honest answer is to keep the free tier, and the free tier will keep working.
