## On the device

Your counters, logs, notes and log photos sit in a local database on your iPhone. TimeSince encrypts it with SQLCipher and keeps the key in the iOS Keychain. The developer has no server holding your counter history.

## If you turn on iCloud backups

One switch in **Settings → Your data → Backups** keeps your iPhones in sync and saves dated, encrypted copies when your data changes. Both are free. You can also choose **Back up now** with the switch off.

If you logged a filter change on your other iPhone, sync brings the two records together. If you accidentally deleted the counter, choose a backup from before the deletion. Restoring it replaces the data on this phone, including any changes since that copy. A new iPhone needs the backup key from iCloud Keychain to read an encrypted backup.

If you are moving to another iPhone, sign in to the same Apple Account and check which copies appear under Backups. A date matters when you want to recover an older entry. If your last good copy was on Monday, restoring it brings back Monday’s records. Logs you added on Tuesday are outside that copy.

## Getting your data out

With Pro you can export and import a plain JSON file. Anyone who can read that file can read the exported records, so choose where you save or send it.

The export can include the notes and photos you attached to a log. The name of the counter alone may tell someone what you track. If you only want to show someone a single entry, check what you are sharing before sending a copy of your records.

## Deleting it

Erase on This Device clears the local data. Erase Everywhere also clears TimeSince’s copies in your iCloud. A file you exported separately stays wherever you saved it.
