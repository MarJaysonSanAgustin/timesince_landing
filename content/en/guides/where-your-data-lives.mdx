Most apps answer this question with a privacy policy, which is a promise. The answer here is closer to an architectural fact: there is no developer-operated server, so there is no central place your counters could be.

## On the device

Your counters, logs, notes and photos sit in a local database on your phone. That database is encrypted with SQLCipher, and the key is generated on your device and kept in the iOS Keychain.

There is no account and no sign-up. Nothing is created on anybody's behalf when you open the app for the first time, which is why there is nothing to breach, sell or subpoena from the developer — not as a policy, but because it does not exist.

## If you turn on sync

Sync is off until you switch it on. When you do, a copy of your data is written to **your own** private iCloud container, under your Apple ID. That container is reachable by you and your devices. You can see the file yourself in the Files app under TimeSince.

This is worth stating clearly because it is the part people assume is a paid feature: iCloud sync is free for everyone. It is not part of Pro, and it never has been.

## Getting your data out

A JSON export gives you the whole thing in a format you can open and keep. This is the export that matters: not a feature you use weekly, but the one that means choosing this app is not a one-way door.

## Deleting it

Two options, and the difference matters. Erase on This Device clears the local database. Erase Everywhere also deletes the iCloud copies. Neither can be undone.

## What this does not protect you from

A locally encrypted database protects data at rest on your phone. It does not protect a JSON export you have emailed to yourself, or a screenshot in your camera roll. And if the device is lost and sync was never on, the data is gone — the absence of a server cuts both ways, and that is the trade you are making.
