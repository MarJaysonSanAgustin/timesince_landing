A request on the feedback board describes it exactly: a household of stuff that has to get done, and a wish to delegate it or be notified when somebody else finishes.

It is the most-voted idea on the board. It is also one the app is not going to do, and the reason matters more than the refusal.

## Why there is no shared household

The developer's reply:

> Sharing a household between people needs an internet service in the middle, and that's a big change to how the app keeps your data private, so we're not adding it right now.

That is the whole architecture in one sentence. There is no account, no sign-up, and no server holding your counters. They are encrypted on your phone, and sync, if you turn it on, goes to your own private iCloud. A shared household would need somewhere in the middle for two people's data to meet, and that somewhere would have to be run by somebody.

So this is a real limitation, and it is the direct cost of a design decision you may well want.

## What actually ends the argument

Here is the part worth noticing: the dispute is almost never about fairness in principle. It is about two people with honest, incompatible memories of when something last happened. Nobody is lying. Everyone remembers their own turns better than the other person's.

That part does not need sharing. It needs a date.

One counter per contested chore, on one phone, on a widget where both of you can see it. Eleven days is not an opinion, and it is remarkably hard to argue with.

## Where this stops working

If you need assignment, notifications, or two people both logging to the same list, this is the wrong tool and no amount of arrangement will fix it. Use something with accounts. That is not a criticism of either app. It is a straight trade between shared state and having no server at all, and you cannot have both.

For the chores themselves, [tracking what recurs on no fixed schedule](/guides/tracking-irregular-chores) covers choosing which ones are worth a counter.
