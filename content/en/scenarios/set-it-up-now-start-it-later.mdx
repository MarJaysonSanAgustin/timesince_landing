The request on the feedback board has a specific example in it, which is what makes it worth a page:

> I'd like to start a habit to "wash the leash" every Saturday, but today is Tuesday. It would be helpful if I could set it up now while I'm thinking of it, choose Saturday as the "Start On" date, and have the reminder begin then.

That is the real shape of the problem. The moment an intention shows up is almost never the moment to act on it, and the gap between the two is where intentions die.

## What Start on does

It sets the day the first reminder arrives. On a Tuesday you can choose a weekly cadence, set Start on to Saturday, and the app stays completely quiet until then. On Saturday the reminder arrives, and after that it repeats weekly.

So the setup costs you nothing in the meantime. No reminder to dismiss on the day you were only planning.

## The part that is easy to miss

Here is the behaviour worth knowing, in the developer's own words:

> once you log it, the next reminder counts from when you actually did it, not from the calendar. If you wash the leash on Sunday instead, the next reminder comes the following Sunday rather than Saturday. That way the app never nags you about something you've just done.

This is the whole philosophy applied to reminders. A calendar-based reminder would fire every Saturday whether or not you did it on Sunday, which means being a day late puts you permanently behind a schedule you did not choose. Counting from your last log instead means the schedule bends to match you.

It is the same reason there are no streaks: the measurement follows what you did, rather than what you were supposed to do.

## What it does not do

Start on moves the reminder, not the counter. The counter measures time since your first log, so it begins when you actually do the thing — which is correct, because a counter that started on a day you did nothing would be counting the wrong interval from the first moment.

And a reminder is still only a nudge. It arrives saying the thing is there whenever you have a free moment. If you want it to be harder to ignore than that, [logging from a widget or a tag](/guides/log-a-habit-without-opening-the-app) does more than a louder notification would.
